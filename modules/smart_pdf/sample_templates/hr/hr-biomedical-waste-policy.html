Biomedical Waste & Safety Policy

Policy No {{document_no}} Effective {{effective_date}} Version {{policy_version}} Review {{review_date}}

1. Purpose & Scope

This policy defines how biomedical waste generated at {{clinic_name}} is to be segregated, handled, stored, transported and disposed of safely. Its purpose is to protect patients, staff, waste handlers, the public and the environment, and to ensure compliance with the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules.

  • Applies to all departments generating clinical waste, including wards, operation theatres, laboratories, pharmacy and diagnostics.
  • Binds all employees, housekeeping staff, contractors and trainees present on the premises.

2. Waste Segregation (Colour-coded Bins)

  • Yellow: human anatomical waste, soiled dressings, contaminated cotton, expired medicines and laboratory waste; sent for incineration or deep burial.
  • Red: contaminated recyclable plastic such as tubing, catheters, IV sets and syringes without needles; treated by autoclaving or sterilisation before recycling.
  • White (translucent, puncture-proof): sharps such as needles, scalpel blades and broken glass ampoules; treated and finally disposed of safely.
  • Blue: broken or discarded glassware and metallic body implants; disinfected before recycling.
  • General non-clinical waste must go into black bins and must never be mixed with biomedical waste.

3. Handling, Storage & Transport

  • Bins must be lined with the correct colour-coded bag, foot-operated where possible, and never filled beyond three-quarters of capacity.
  • Each bag and container must carry the biohazard symbol and the required labelling before removal.
  • Segregated waste is moved along designated routes to the central storage area and must not be stored on-site for more than the permitted period.
  • Trolleys used for waste transport must be dedicated for the purpose and cleaned and disinfected after each use.

4. Staff Responsibilities & PPE

  • Waste must be segregated at the point of generation by the person generating it; segregation is not to be deferred to housekeeping.
  • Handlers must wear appropriate personal protective equipment, including gloves, masks, aprons and closed footwear.
  • Hand hygiene must be performed before and after handling waste, even when gloves have been worn.
  • Sharps must never be recapped by hand and must be discarded immediately into the white puncture-proof container.

5. Needle-stick & Spill Management

  • In case of a needle-stick or sharps injury, wash the area immediately with soap and running water and do not squeeze the wound.
  • Report the incident at once to the supervisor and the infection-control officer and initiate post-exposure evaluation and prophylaxis as advised.
  • For spills of blood or body fluids, cordon off the area, apply an appropriate disinfectant, clean from the outside inwards and dispose of the material as yellow-category waste.
  • Every needle-stick injury and significant spill must be documented in the incident register.

6. Training & Records

  • All clinical and housekeeping staff receive induction and periodic refresher training on biomedical waste management.
  • Records of waste quantities, colour-category-wise, are maintained and reconciled with the authorised disposal facility.
  • Records of training, immunisation of handlers, incidents and audits are retained for the period prescribed under the rules.

7. Regulatory Compliance

  • The organisation operates under a valid authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board and engages only an authorised common treatment facility for disposal.
  • Annual returns and other statutory reports are filed within the prescribed timelines.
  • Non-compliance by any individual or contractor will be treated seriously and dealt with under the applicable disciplinary and contractual provisions.

Management reserves the right to amend or interpret this policy consistent with the governing regulations, with changes communicated through official channels. This policy shall be read together with the infection-control protocols of {{clinic_name}}.
